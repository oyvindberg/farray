## The headline: fourteen stages, warm

The pipeline from the introduction — `flatMap`, `filter`, `map`, `zip`, `zipWithIndex`, `foldLeft`,
twice over. Every collection runs the identical code; the FArray entry differs by the one word:

## Cold: no warmup lap

Because the specialization is in the bytecode, there is no lap where a boxing collection is briefly
ahead:

<BenchChart
  cls="ColdPipelineIntBenchmark"

## Streaming: constant memory without the streaming tax

The chunk-granular <Link to="/fusion/sources">source model</Link>, against the JVM's usual ways of
processing data you don't want in memory:

## The chain that used to collapse

The reason "compile the pipeline" beats "hope the JIT saves you", preserved as a benchmark: this
exact shape once ran at 0.055× of IArray when a trailing structural call silently disabled escape
analysis on a spliced loop — <Link to="/operations/flatmap">the full story</Link>. Fused, the
question doesn't exist:

<BenchChart
  cls="FlatMapFilterTakeStrBenchmark"
  caption="flatMap → filter → take on String elements. The trailing take is O(1) for FArray (a lazy SliceNode), and the fused pipeline leads at every size — with no dependence on the caller's method size or call-site history."
/>

## JSON projection queries

The byte-source numbers live with their scanners and rivals on{" "}
<Link to="/fusion/json">the JSON integration page</Link> — headline: 1.6× past jsoniter-scala's
hand-written reader loop at ~1 B/op, 4–9× past the AST parsers, and 2.5× on selective queries where
records are abandoned mid-scan.
