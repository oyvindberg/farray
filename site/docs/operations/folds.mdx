<p className="lede">
  A fold is the purest boxing benchmark there is: no output collection, no structural work — just one
  lambda applied per element into an accumulator. Whatever a library's per-element crossing costs,
  a fold is <em>nothing but</em> that crossing, a hundred thousand times. Which makes this page the
  cleanest view of the thesis: FArray's unboxing lives in method signatures, everyone else's lives in
  the JIT's good mood.
</p>

## The microbenchmark that can't tell anyone apart

Start with the honest baseline, because it frames everything after it. A *lone* fold is the case
every JIT handles: one call site, one lambda class, the shared generic `foldLeft` inlines and
specializes, and everyone's loop ends up load-add-load-add. Measured that way, FArray and a bare
`int[]` sit together at the hardware ceiling — within 1% at every size — and that tie is the correct
result, not a disappointment:

If folds ended here, no library would have anything to sell."

The interesting question is why that tie doesn't survive contact with a codebase.

## The part that matters: folds in real methods

Competitors' primitive folds run unboxed *because* the JIT inlines the shared generic `foldLeft`
into the call site and specializes it — the tie above is rented, paid for out of the method's
inlining budget. Put **several folds in one method** — a completely ordinary reporting or
aggregation method — and the budget runs out: the shared fold collapses back to its generic boxed
signature, one call site at a time, as the method grows. We measured the collapse beginning at four
folds per method on C2 and around twelve on Graal.

FArray's fold surface is a tiny call to a compiled-once leaf — deliberately *not* inlined — so the
sixth fold in a method compiles exactly like the first. The benchmark runs six reference folds in one
measured method, and the sweep shows the whole arc:

<BenchChart
  cls="FoldRefPollutedBenchmark"
  caption="Six reference folds in ONE measured method. At 1k the competitors' folds are still inlined and FArray's shared-leaf call overhead shows — an honest 0.47× cell. If you take one number away from this page, take the pair: 1.00× alone, 3.5× embedded. The first is
what microbenchmarks measure; the second is what your codebase runs.

## scan: the op that taught us about loop shape

`scanLeft` emits every intermediate accumulator — a fold that writes its state out as it goes. It
also carries this library's favorite measure-don't-assume scar: the first implementation carried the
accumulator *through the output array* (`out(o) = op(out(o - 1), v)`), which reads back a value
written one iteration ago — a store-to-load dependency on the loop's serial chain. That one shape
decision ran at 0.22–0.40× of a raw array. Moving the accumulator into a register local — the change
is two lines — made it 4×, to a dead tie with `IArray`. Nothing about dispatch, boxing, or the
tree: just where a loop-carried value lives.
